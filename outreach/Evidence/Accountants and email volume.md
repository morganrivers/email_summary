# Accountants and email volume

The clearest quantified pain found anywhere in this research.

## Numbers

- Accountants and bookkeepers handle **100–200 emails a day** in ordinary periods.
- Busy season multiplies that, while the same teams absorb several times their normal return volume under fixed deadlines.
- The reported consequences are the ones a drafting assistant addresses directly: delays, errors, and time lost to interruption when uninterrupted review time is what the work requires.

## The framing that is already in their trade press

Two things their own publications say, which you would otherwise have to argue for:

1. **Email is a compliance channel, not just a productivity one.** Accounting Today has run this argument during tax season. It means the confidentiality pitch is not foreign to this audience; it is already their vocabulary.
2. **Clients judge accountants on communication, not competence.** Repeated in practice-management writing: clients rarely complain about technical skill, they complain about how they were communicated with. A tool that makes replies faster and better written is therefore a client-retention tool, not an efficiency toy. That is a stronger sale than time saved.

## Why this audience is underrated

Nobody is building privacy-first AI for accountants, because accountants are not glamorous and do not appear on Hacker News. They have the volume pain, the confidentiality duty, the seasonal deadline, the money, and in Germany they sit inside §203 StGB alongside lawyers and doctors. See [[German professional secrecy holders]].

## Timing

Approach in the shoulder season. During busy season nobody will answer, and a bad first impression during a crunch is permanent.
