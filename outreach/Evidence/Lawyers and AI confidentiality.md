# Lawyers and AI confidentiality

The answer to "do lawyers complain about this in a way I can find?" Yes, but not as complaints. As rules, which is better.

## United States: ABA Formal Opinion 512

Issued 2024-07-29 by the ABA Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility. First formal ethics guidance on generative AI. The parts that matter to you:

- Lawyers are **responsible for knowing how a GAI tool uses their data** and must put adequate safeguards in place against unauthorised disclosure to third parties.
- Lawyers should obtain **clients' informed consent** before putting client confidences into a GAI tool, and **boilerplate consent in an engagement letter is not adequate**.
- Before inputting anything, a lawyer must weigh the likelihood of disclosure or unauthorised access, the sensitivity of the information, the difficulty of implementing safeguards, and the effect of those safeguards on the representation.
- The opinion notes that some AI in email and research is already accepted practice, but that GAI tools are not yet required for competence.

Read what that does for you. A lawyer cannot satisfy 512 by trusting a vendor's promise, because the standard is knowing how the data is used and safeguarding it. An architecture where the vendor *cannot* read the content is not a nice-to-have under this opinion; it is the cleanest way to satisfy it. Meanwhile "we don't train on your data" in a ToS is, on its face, not enough.

State bars have followed with their own guidance, generally consistent, sometimes stricter.

## Germany: §203 StGB

Stronger, because it is criminal law rather than professional discipline. Covered in detail in [[German professional secrecy holders]]. Summary: disclosure of a client secret by a Berufsgeheimnisträger is punishable; a data-processing agreement alone does not cure it; involving a service provider is lawful only if it is necessary for proper practice, the provider is bound to secrecy **in text form** and informed of criminal liability, and the professional selects and supervises the provider carefully. German commentary on AI specifically says confidential data must not leave the protected environment.

## The volume complaint, separately

Documented but old and soft: surveys putting most legal professionals in a state of information overload, most saying they cannot find the right information at the right time, and practitioner accounts of a daily barrage of scheduling and deadline mail in which important things get lost. Useful as the reason they will look at a product. Not useful as a differentiator, since every legal-tech product claims it.
