# Journalists and their sources

**Status:** not started

Little money, disproportionate credibility. A journalist who trusts your tool is a reference the professional branch will believe.

## Threat model, in their words

Source protection, and it is not abstract: a leaked source can be prosecuted. This audience already runs Signal, SecureDrop, OnionShare, and already thinks in adversaries and threat models rather than in features.

## The catch

They mostly should not be using an AI email assistant on source correspondence at all, and the honest position is to say so. Where you fit is the other 90% of a journalist's inbox: PR, editors, scheduling, admin. Saying "do not point this at your SecureDrop" out loud is the kind of thing that earns you standing with this group and with [[Privacy-conscious technical users]] at the same time.

## Where they are

- **[[Freedom of the Press Foundation]]** — freedom.press. Digital security training team, guides, and the annual journalist's digital security checklist. They evaluate tools and their opinion carries
- **Netzwerk Recherche** — netzwerkrecherche.org, German investigative journalism network, Berlin-adjacent
- **[[Press and newsletters]]** — netzpolitik.org is both press and community here
- Investigative journalism conferences: Dataharvest (EU), NICAR (US)

## Angle

Not a sale. A credibility loop: the security community advises journalists, journalists write about tools, professionals read the coverage.
