# Lawyers

**Status:** not started

Solo and small-firm practitioners, not BigLaw. BigLaw buys through procurement and pilots; solos buy with a credit card.

## Threat model, in their words

Not privacy as a value. Liability. A lawyer who puts client confidences into a tool that trains on them, or that a vendor employee can read, has an ethics problem, potentially a malpractice problem, and in Germany a criminal one ([[German professional secrecy holders]]).

The governing text in the US is **ABA Formal Opinion 512** (2024-07-29), and it is unusually useful to you: it says lawyers must know how a GAI tool uses their data and must put safeguards in place, and it says **boilerplate consent in an engagement letter is not adequate**. That is a written, citable reason why "we don't train on your data" in a ToS is insufficient and why an architectural answer is worth more. See [[Lawyers and AI confidentiality]].

## The pain that is not privacy

Email volume. Documented repeatedly: seven in ten legal professionals report being inundated, and the daily complaint is scheduling, deadlines, and important things lost in the pile. This is the pain that makes them look; confidentiality is the objection that stops them buying. You answer both, which is unusual.

## Angle that works

Do not sell an AI email assistant to lawyers. Offer an answer to Formal Opinion 512. The product is the same; the framing determines whether the bar rules are an objection or the reason to buy.
